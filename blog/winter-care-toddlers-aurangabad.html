Winter Care for Toddlers: Clothing, Food and Colds

Simple ways to keep your little one warm, well fed and healthy through Aurangabad's cool winter months.

Photo by Aditi Panatu on Unsplash

November 9, 2026

Winter in Aurangabad arrives gently. Mornings and evenings turn cool and the air becomes dry, while afternoons can still feel warm and sunny. For little ones this means a day of changing temperatures — chilly at school drop-off, warm by lunchtime and cool again after sunset. Our teachers at TinyTots have put together simple, practical ideas to help your child stay comfortable, eat well and bounce back from the sniffles that often come with the season.

Dress in Layers, Not Bulk

Because the temperature changes so much through the day, layers work far better than one heavy jacket. A cotton vest or T-shirt, a full-sleeve top and a light sweater or zip-up jacket can be added or removed as the day warms up.

  • Mornings and evenings: a sweater or jacket, socks and comfortable closed shoes. A soft cap or scarf helps on breezy two-wheeler rides.
  • Afternoons: let children take off a layer when they are running around, so they do not get sweaty and then chilled.
  • For preschool: choose jackets with big zips or buttons that small hands can manage, and write your child's name inside every layer — sweaters have a way of going missing!

A simple check: feel the back of your child's neck. If it is warm and dry, they are comfortable. Cold hands alone are not a sign that they need more clothes.

Warm, Seasonal Food from the Indian Kitchen

Winter brings some of the best seasonal food to our markets. Fresh green vegetables such as methi, palak and peas, carrots for gajar dishes, guavas, oranges and amla are all easy to find. Traditional winter favourites in Maharashtrian homes — bajra bhakri, til and gur, warm dal and vegetable khichdi — are filling and comforting for little ones too.

Simple ideas for the tiffin and home:

  • A small flask of warm vegetable soup or dal on cooler days.
  • Methi or palak paratha with curd.
  • Orange or guava pieces as a mid-morning snack.
  • Til-gul laddoo in small portions, cut or crumbled for younger children.

For more tiffin inspiration, see our post on healthy lunch ideas that children actually love.

Keep the Water Bottle Going

Children often drink less water when the weather is cool, yet the dry winter air means they still need plenty. Send a full water bottle to school every day and check how much comes home. Warm water, homemade soups and milk all count. Offer water regularly at home too, especially after play — little ones rarely ask for it on their own.

Care for Dry Skin and Lips

The dry air in winter can leave children with rough cheeks, cracked lips and itchy skin. A few simple habits help:

  • Use lukewarm, not very hot, water for baths and keep bath time short.
  • Pat the skin dry and apply a gentle moisturiser or a little coconut oil while the skin is still slightly damp.
  • Remind children not to lick their lips, and apply a plain lip balm before they head out.

If your child has a rash that does not settle, or skin that cracks or bleeds, please show it to your paediatrician.

Colds and Coughs: Prevention and Gentle Care

Coughs and colds are very common in young children, especially once they start spending time with other children. Most colds are caused by viruses, which means antibiotics do not help them. The good news is that most children recover on their own within a week or so with rest, fluids and lots of cuddles.

Everyday habits that help reduce the spread of germs:

  • Washing hands with soap and water before meals, after using the toilet and after coming home from outside.
  • Teaching children to cough or sneeze into their elbow.
  • Keeping a child at home to rest when they have a fever or are clearly unwell, so they can recover and other children stay healthy.

Please do not give cough or cold medicines on your own. India's Health Ministry has advised that cough and cold medicines should not be given to children under two years of age, and for older children too it is best to ask your paediatrician before giving any medicine. Do contact a doctor promptly if your child has a high or long-lasting fever, fast or difficult breathing, unusual sleepiness, is not drinking well, or simply seems more unwell than usual. It is also a good time to check with your paediatrician that your child's vaccinations are up to date.

Sunshine, Play and Safe Warmth at Home

Cool weather is no reason to stay indoors all day. Winter mornings and late afternoons in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar are often lovely for outdoor play — a walk in the park, a game of catch or a little time on the terrace in the sun. Running and climbing keep children warm and help them sleep well at night. On chilly evenings, our list of fun home activities for toddlers has plenty of indoor ideas.

At night, a cotton layer under a warm blanket is usually enough. If you use a room heater, keep it well away from bedding, curtains and little hands, and never leave a coal sigri or angithi burning in a closed room — it can release harmful fumes.

Enjoy the Season Together

With a few layers, warm home-cooked food and good hand-washing habits, winter can be one of the most enjoyable times of the year for young children. If you have questions about your child's winter routine, our teachers at TinyTots are always happy to talk.
