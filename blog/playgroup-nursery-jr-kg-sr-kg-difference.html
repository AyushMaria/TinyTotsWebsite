Playgroup, Nursery, Jr KG and Sr KG: What Is the Difference?

A simple guide to the four preschool years — what each stage is for, and how to choose the right one for your child.

Photo by Lucas Alexander on Unsplash

October 27, 2026

When parents in Aurangabad start looking at preschools, the first question is often a simple one: "Which class should my child join?" Playgroup, Nursery, Jr KG and Sr KG can sound like four different schools, but they are really four steps on one gentle journey. Each year builds on the one before, and each has its own purpose. Here is a plain-language guide from our teachers to help you understand what happens at every stage.

Playgroup (Ages 2–3): Learning to Be Away from Home

Playgroup is usually a child's very first experience of a classroom. The main goal is not letters or numbers. It is helping a little one feel safe and happy in a new place, with new adults and other children.

A typical playgroup day is full of rhymes, music, sand and water play, building blocks, simple art and lots of time outdoors. Through this play, children learn to share, wait for their turn, follow a short routine and use words to ask for what they need. If your child is just beginning to talk in short sentences and is curious about other children, playgroup is a lovely first step.

Nursery (Ages 3–4): Curiosity and Early Skills

In Nursery, children are ready for a little more structure. They still learn mainly through play, but the activities start to build early skills on purpose. Sorting objects by colour and size, counting things around the classroom, listening to stories and recognising a few letters by their sounds all happen here.

Children also work on fine motor skills — holding a crayon properly, tearing and pasting paper, threading beads — which prepare their hands for writing later. Just as importantly, they learn to express their feelings and become a little more independent, like opening their own tiffin or putting on their shoes.

Jr KG (Ages 4–5): Getting Ready to Read and Count

Jr KG, also called LKG in many schools, is where early literacy and numeracy really take shape. Children begin to connect letters with sounds, read simple words, write letters and numbers, and understand ideas like more and less, bigger and smaller, and simple patterns.

The day becomes a little longer and more focused, but good Jr KG classrooms still use songs, games, stories and hands-on materials so that learning feels joyful rather than like homework. Children at this age also love show-and-tell and small group projects, which build confidence and speaking skills.

Sr KG (Ages 5–6): The Bridge to Class 1

Sr KG, often called UKG, prepares children for the move to primary school. They read short sentences, write simple words, add and subtract small numbers using objects, and start thinking more logically through puzzles and questions.

Equally important is school readiness: sitting and listening for longer, following two- or three-step instructions, managing their own belongings and solving small problems with friends. A child who leaves Sr KG confident and curious will find Class 1 far less overwhelming.

How to Choose the Right Class for Your Child

Age is the usual starting point, but it is not the only thing to consider. Ask yourself:

  • Is my child comfortable away from me for a few hours? If not yet, a playgroup routine can help build that comfort gently.
  • How does my child communicate? Children who can share simple needs in words usually settle faster.
  • What has my child done before? A child starting school for the first time at four may need a few weeks of extra settling, even in Jr KG.
  • What does the school's age cut-off say? Schools follow age cut-off dates for each class, and the rules for Class 1 admission are set by the state. Always check the latest cut-off with the school before you apply.

If you are unsure, talk to the teachers. A short visit, where your child can explore the classroom while you ask questions, often makes the decision clear.

One Journey, Four Happy Steps

The best preschool years do not rush children from one skill to the next. They let each child grow at their own pace, with plenty of play, kindness and encouragement along the way. Whether your little one is two or five, there is a right place for them to begin.
